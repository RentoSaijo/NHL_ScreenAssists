The adjusted odds ratio is —. The mixed-effects logistic model adjusts for season, competition, strength, shot location, release type, broad play context, preceding pass locations, and both teams, with shooter, goalie, and game random intercepts.

Screen-on-pass cases, posts, deflection releases, and distinct mixed-screen, defensive-screen, one-timer/screen, and rebound classifications are outside this primary contrast. CSA’s observed population should not be equated with all NHL shot attempts. Residual confounding and annotation choices can affect the result.

The fit places the goalie random-intercept variance at zero. We retain this boundary estimate. The reported model-based intervals use fixed-effect uncertainty with variance components held at their fitted values; they do not incorporate every source of model or annotation uncertainty.

Where this fits in hockey research

Manual screen-assist work by Justin Cait already recognizes these contributions. Tracking-based traffic research studies players in shooting lanes, while net-visibility research brings pose into related visibility questions. Our contribution is a reproducible public-replay attribution pipeline with independent player-level evaluation and inspectable results.

Public goal replays cannot tell us how frequently a player screens on non-goals, whether the same detector would perform equally well on them, or how many additional goals the screen creates. The separate CSA outcome analysis supplies evidence about association; multiplying that association by leaderboard counts would not yield a defensible causal player value.
